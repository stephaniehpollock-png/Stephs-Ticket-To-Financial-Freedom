Why I'm building this

Teens deserve skills they actually want to use.

I work with teens as a counselor. Every week I watch DBT and CBT skills change how a teen handles a hard moment: a panic that passes faster, a fight with a parent that doesn't blow up, a night that gets survived. These skills work.

The hard part is getting teens to practice them. Worksheets feel like homework. Most mental health apps are built for adults, and teens delete them within days. Meanwhile, the same teens put hours into games every week, competing, leveling up and coming back every day because they want to.

So I started asking: what if practicing coping skills felt like that? What if a teen earned points for using TIPP before a test, kept a streak going for practicing every day, and climbed a league with other teens working on the same things?

That's HeadGame. It isn't therapy and it doesn't replace treatment. It's a way to put real skills in teens' hands early, at a price every family can afford, and to give treatment programs one more way to help their clients practice.

What I believe

The principles behind every decision.

Skills, not lectures

Teens learn by doing. Every skill in HeadGame takes 2 to 5 minutes, and the biggest rewards go to using skills in real life.

The teen brain is a strength

Following Dr. Daniel Siegel's Brainstorm, I see adolescence as a time of growth, creativity and connection, not something to just survive.

Safe to be honest

Teens open up when they feel safe. HeadGame is anonymous, teens control what parents see, and crisis help is always one tap away.

Compete on effort, never on mood

Nobody gets ranked on how they feel. Points come from practicing, so showing up is what wins.

Help before crisis

Coping skills shouldn't only reach teens after things get serious. Prevention should be affordable for every family.

Families learn together

When a teen invites them, parents learn the same skills, so home becomes a place where those skills get used.

Building in public

Where HeadGame is now.

  1. NowDesigning the product. Skill pillars, points, safety and privacy, and Family Mode are mapped out.
  2. NextWriting the clinical content. Every DBT and CBT lesson written and reviewed by a licensed clinician.
  3. ThenProgram pilot. A first treatment program tries HeadGame with its clients and shares feedback.
  4. ThenFamily beta. A small group of families tests the app before launch.
  5. LaterPublic launch, then a young adult version for ages 18 to 25.
