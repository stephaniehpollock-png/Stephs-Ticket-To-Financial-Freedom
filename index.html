How it works

Your brain is built for this.

Inspired by Dr. Daniel Siegel's book Brainstorm, HeadGame treats the teen brain as a strength, not a problem. Every teen starts by learning what's happening inside their head, then picks the skills that help.

A daily check-in, never scored

Revved upTry a skill that calms your body fast, like TIPP or paced breathing.
In your windowA good time to learn something new.
Shut downTry a skill that gently gets you moving, like Opposite Action.

DBT: 5 pillars

Mindfulness Distress Tolerance Emotion Regulation Interpersonal Effectiveness Walking the Middle Path

CBT: 7 pillars

Know Your Brain Calm Your Body Name It to Tame It Thinking Traps Thought Detective Take Action Safety & Strength

Points for practice, never for mood

10points for completing a skill
20points for using a skill in real life
100bonus points and a badge for finishing a pillar

Weekly leagues rank teens by practice points, only against teens their own age. A daily streak rewards showing up. When a streak breaks, HeadGame says "Coming back is a skill too."

Family Mode, if your teen wants it

Teens choose whether to invite a parent and exactly what that parent can see. Parents learn the same skills on their own path, plus tools like "connect, then redirect," so the whole family speaks the same language.

Your parent will see: streak and badges. You can change this anytime.

Safety & privacy

Safe to be honest here.

  • Anonymous. App-made usernames. No real names, photos or locations.
  • Teens 13+ only. Minors only ever compete with minors their own age.
  • No private messages. Only preset cheers, so no one can contact your teen.
  • The teen controls sharing. Nothing is shared with parents by default.
  • Built to be HIPAA-compliant.

HeadGame is a self-guided skills tool. It isn't therapy and doesn't replace treatment.

If a teen types something that sounds like a crisis

HeadGame shows crisis help right away: call or text 988, text HOME to 741741, or call 911. It also says plainly that no one at HeadGame reads their entries, so they know to reach a real person now.

Skill of the week

TIPP: calm your body when feelings get really big

A DBT distress tolerance skill. When emotions spike, your thinking brain goes offline. TIPP works through your body to bring it back.

  1. TTemperatureCold water on your face for 30 seconds.
  2. IIntense exerciseJumping jacks or a fast walk for a few minutes.
  3. PPaced breathingBreathe out longer than you breathe in.
  4. PPaired muscle relaxationTense, then release, as you breathe out.

Check with a doctor before using cold water if you have a heart condition or an eating disorder.
